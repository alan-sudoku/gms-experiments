## 0. Adversarial reading of the prompt

Five weaknesses the plan sets out to test. They drive the claims in section 1.

1. "Transport-governed" does not separate the two classes. Photon attenuation and neutron absorption (Class I examples 1 and 2) are linear transport problems, and their compensation law (required areal mass ∝ 1/coefficient) is the same law as Fourier or Ohm conduction (required cross-section ∝ 1/coefficient).
2. The "findings so far" (three null results) may be one theorem counted three times. If function is monotonic in the coefficient and the incumbent may use the same geometry, the candidate can never win on function. It can only win on the resource side (density × embodied entropy). That is a material index, not a geometry effect.
3. Each Class I example has an operating regime where it fails: photons in the Compton range (per mass), bulk neutron shielding with a moderator, storage at short discharge times, permanent magnets where volume is free or where shape anisotropy supplies coercivity.
4. The stated numbers mix bases. The "25–30×" for tungsten versus carbon is a total-attenuation ratio dominated by Compton scattering in carbon; the quoted Z⁴/E³ law does not produce it. The "1–4 eV/atom versus 0.002 eV/atom" compares energy per transferred electron with energy per carbon atom.
5. The scarce element is misidentified in at least one example: boron, the most used neutron absorber, is not a scarce element.

## 2. Derivations

Symbols: c coefficient; κ = c_s/c_g ≥ 1; Γ geometry; 𝒢 admissible geometry set; ρ density (kg/m³); e embodied exergy destroyed per kg (J/kg); e_Σ = e/T₀ (J/(K·kg)); τ service time under load (s); T₀ reference temperature (K); subscripts s incumbent, g candidate.

### D1. Monotonicity lemma (G2.1)

Assume F(c, Γ) is non-decreasing in c for every Γ, and both materials may use any Γ ∈ 𝒢. For c_g ≤ c_s: F(c_g, Γ) ≤ F(c_s, Γ) for each Γ, hence sup over 𝒢 preserves the inequality. Consequence: at equal geometry the candidate never exceeds the incumbent in function. A candidate can win the Σ comparison only through the resource terms (ρ·e_Σ·V, or mass where mass is the function's own constraint) or through a 𝒢 that differs between materials. The lemma fails for functions that target a value and not an extremum (sheet resistance of a heater, matched absorbers), and where a second coefficient of x enters with the opposite sign (transparency versus conduction).

### D2. Separability lemma and the fairness-rule argument (G2.2)

Assume (i) F = c·S(Γ) with S independent of material; (ii) Σ_init = e_Σ·ρ·V(Γ); (iii) the operating term at equal F is material-independent, or is itself a function of c·S only. Let V*(S) = min{V(Γ): Γ ∈ 𝒢, S(Γ) ≥ S}. The requirement F ≥ F_req becomes S ≥ F_req/c, so

Σ*_init(m) = e_Σ,m·ρ_m·V*(F_req/c_m).

If V*(S) ∝ S^n (homogeneous of degree n):

Σ*_init,g/Σ*_init,s = (e_g·ρ_g/e_s·ρ_s)·κ^n.

Geometry appears only through the exponent n. The candidate wins if and only if c_g^n/(ρ_g·e_g) > c_s^n/(ρ_s·e_s). This is a material index. Values of n: 1 for a prismatic conductor of fixed length (V = A·L, S = A/L); 1 for the optimum rectangular fin (D5); 1/2 in width for a thick skin-limited conductor of fixed thickness (D4).

With an operating term that trades against volume (Kelvin's law), per conductor of length L carrying RMS current I:

Σ(A) = e_Σ·ρ·A·L + I²·L·τ/(σ·A·T₀).

dΣ/dA = 0 gives A* = I·sqrt(τ/(σ·ρ·e_Σ·T₀)) = I·sqrt(τ/(σ·ρ·e)), and Σ* = 2·I·L·sqrt(ρ·e_Σ·τ/(σ·T₀)) = 2·I·L·sqrt(ρ·e·τ/σ)/T₀. At the optimum the embodied and operating terms are equal (D = 1). The ratio Σ*_g/Σ*_s = sqrt[(e_g·ρ_g/σ_g)/(e_s·ρ_s/σ_s)] contains no geometry and no duty. Break-even: e_g/e_s = (ρ_s/σ_s)/(ρ_g/σ_g).

The fairness-rule argument in one paragraph. Under the rule both materials optimise over the same 𝒢. By D1 the candidate cannot win on function. By D2, where function separates as c·S(Γ), every geometric gain multiplies both sides and cancels, and the ranking is fixed by a material index with exponent n. Geometry can change the ranking only through three doors: (a) a regime change that alters n for one material and not the other (thin versus thick relative to a penetration depth; bounded between κ and κ^(1/2), so it narrows the gap and never reverses it); (b) an envelope that truncates 𝒢 (the larger S needed by g does not fit); (c) a material-dependent 𝒢 (process limits). Two further doors lie outside the lemma's assumptions: a mechanism switch that removes c from the limiting resistance (G2.7) and spatial decoupling of two functions that x couples (G2.8). The null results reported in the prompt are what D1 and D2 predict for separable, monotonic, solid-limited cases; they do not show that substitution never pays.

### D3. Duty rule (G2.4)

Σ_g − Σ_s = −β·Σ_init,s + ε·Σ̇_s·τ, with β = 1 − Σ_init,g/Σ_init,s and ε = Σ̇_g/Σ̇_s − 1. Substitution pays if and only if ε·D < β, D = Σ̇_s·τ/Σ_init,s. For a conductor sized at current density J against its Kelvin optimum J*, D = (J/J*)². At equal cross-section A: ε = κ − 1, and the condition becomes J²·τ < (e_s·ρ_s − e_g·ρ_g)/(1/σ_g − 1/σ_s), which has no solution when e_g·ρ_g ≥ e_s·ρ_s.

Series-resistance form (bottleneck elsewhere): R = R_x(c) + R_shared. With φ = R_x,s/R_s at the incumbent, the same-geometry penalty is R_g/R_s − 1 = φ·(κ^n − 1).

### D4. Compensation-price scaling laws, conduction (G1.3, G1.4)

DC or bulk heat conduction, fixed length: G = c·A/L. Equal G: A_g/A_s = κ, V_g/V_s = κ, m_g/m_s = κ·ρ_g/ρ_s. No operating price.

Skin effect. δ = sqrt(2/(ω·μ₀·σ)) (m). One-sided field, plate thickness t, ξ = t/δ: R_ac/R_dc = F(ξ) = ξ·(sinh 2ξ + sin 2ξ)/(cosh 2ξ − cos 2ξ). Isolated strip with field on both faces: the same F with ξ = t/(2δ). Limits: F → 1 + 4ξ⁴/45 as ξ → 0; F → ξ as ξ → ∞. Thick limit per unit width: R′ = 1/(σ·δ) = sqrt(ω·μ₀/(2σ)) ∝ σ^(−1/2). Same outer geometry, thick: R_g/R_s = κ^(1/2). Compensation by width: w_g/w_s = κ^(1/2). R_ac of a single layer has a minimum at ξ = π/2 where R_ac is 0.917 of the thick-limit value. Subdivision (foil, litz, transposition) returns both materials to F ≈ 1 and to n = 1, at the price of fill factor: conductor envelope volume ∝ κ/(fill). Utilisation: η_u = 1/F.

### D5. Compensation-price scaling laws, convective heat transfer and viscous flow (G1.5, G1.6)

Plate fin, thickness t, height L, adiabatic tip: m = sqrt(2h/(k·t)) (1/m), η_f = tanh(mL)/(mL), heat flow per unit width q′ = θ_b·sqrt(2·h·k·t)·tanh(mL) (W/m). Equal η_f at equal L and h: t_g/t_s = κ; fin mass ratio κ·ρ_g/ρ_s. Thick limit (mL ≫ 1): q′ ∝ k^(1/2), penalty κ^(1/2).

Optimum rectangular fin for a given profile area A_p = t·L: maximise q′; stationarity gives tanh β = 3β·sech²β, β = mL = 1.4192; then A_p = 0.504·(q′/θ_b)³/(h²·k). Fin mass per unit width for a given duty ∝ ρ/k (n = 1), with t ∝ 1/k and L unchanged. Mass crossover against Cu: k_g = ρ_g·k_s/ρ_s ≈ 120 W/(m·K) for Al-alloy density (MEMORY inputs). Below that conductivity an aluminium alloy fin is heavier than a copper fin of equal duty.

Flow blockage at fixed pitch p: gap b = p − t. Laminar parallel plates: ΔP = 12·μ·L_f·U/b² and U = V̇/(N·b·W), so ΔP ∝ b⁻³ and P_pump = ΔP·V̇ ∝ b⁻³ at fixed V̇.

Compensation by flow. Turbulent duct: Nu ∝ Re^0.8, friction factor ∝ Re^(−0.25), so ΔP ∝ U^1.75, P ∝ U^2.75, P ∝ h^(2.75/0.8) = h^3.44. Developing laminar boundary layer: h ∝ U^(1/2), drag ∝ U^(3/2), P ∝ U^(5/2), P ∝ h⁵. Fully developed laminar: Nu constant, h independent of U; only the caloric resistance 1/(ṁ·c_p) responds, R_th = 1/(ṁ·c_p·(1 − exp(−NTU))), NTU = h·A·η_f/(ṁ·c_p). All pumping power is dissipated: Σ̇_pump = P_pump/T₀.

Viscous flow. Hagen–Poiseuille: ΔP = 128·μ·L·Q/(π·D⁴), P = ΔP·Q. The only material property is the fluid's viscosity. Branching optimum (Murray): D_parent³ = Σ D_child³. No coefficient of x appears.

### D6. Compensation-price scaling laws, electrocatalysis (G1.7, G2.6)

Butler–Volmer per real area with roughness factor R_f: j_geo = R_f·j₀·[exp(α_a·F·η/(R·T)) − exp(−α_c·F·η/(R·T))]. Tafel branch: η = b·log₁₀(j_geo/(R_f·j₀)), b = 2.303·R·T/(α·F) (V/decade; 118.3 mV at α = 0.5, 298.15 K). At equal j_geo and equal b: Δη = η_g − η_s = b·log₁₀(κ·R_f,s/R_f,g). Compensation by area alone: R_f,g/R_f,s = κ. Compensation by overpotential alone: Δη = b·log₁₀κ, operating price j·Δη (W/m²).

Porous electrode, linearised kinetics, ionic conductivity σ_ion (S/m), volumetric area a (m²/m³), thickness L: φ″ = φ/λ², λ = sqrt(σ_ion·R·T/(a·j₀·F)) (m). Utilisation η_u = tanh(L/λ)/(L/λ). Effective exchange current per geometric area: j₀,eff = j₀·a·λ·tanh(L/λ); thin limit j₀·a·L; thick limit sqrt(a·j₀·σ_ion·R·T/F) ∝ j₀^(1/2). In the Tafel regime the thick-electrode apparent Tafel slope doubles (stated, not derived here; UNSURE as to the exact conditions).

Break-even overpotential against a scarce catalyst. Per geometric area: embodied term of x is e_x·m″_x/T₀ (m″_x loading, kg/m²); operating penalty of the candidate is j·Δη·τ/T₀. Break-even Δη_be = e_x·m″_x/(j·τ), neglecting the candidate's own embodied term (which only lowers Δη_be).

Like-for-like condition. A catalyst that is thermodynamically unstable in the incumbent's electrolyte (acidic membrane cell versus alkaline cell) fails a threshold, not a transport comparison. That pair is Type T (G1.2) and the stability window is a scenario input.

### D7. Photon attenuation (G3.1–G3.5)

Narrow beam: T = exp(−(μ/ρ)·x), x areal density (kg/m² or g/cm²). Mass ratio for equal T: m_C/m_W = (μ/ρ)_W/(μ/ρ)_C. Thickness ratio: (μ/ρ·ρ)_W/(μ/ρ·ρ)_C.

Compton part: (μ/ρ)_C ≈ N_A·(Z/A)·σ_KN(E), with the Klein–Nishina total cross-section per electron, k = E/(m_e·c²):

σ_KN = 2π·r_e²·{ (1+k)/k²·[2(1+k)/(1+2k) − ln(1+2k)/k] + ln(1+2k)/(2k) − (1+3k)/(1+2k)² }.

Limit k → 0: σ_KN → 8π·r_e²/3 = 0.6652 b. Because Z/A is 0.4995 for C and 0.4025 for W, pure Compton scattering makes carbon 1.24× better per unit mass than tungsten; the measured ratio near 1.0 at 1–2 MeV is Compton with a small photoelectric and pair remainder in W.

Jensen. For any thickness distribution with mean x̄: E[exp(−μx)] ≥ exp(−μ·x̄), equality only for uniform x. Uniform distribution on [0, 2x̄]: E = (1 − exp(−2μx̄))/(2μx̄).

Broad beam: transmitted energy T_E = B_E(μx)·exp(−μx), buildup B_E ≥ 1 and larger for low Z. Extra areal mass from buildup: Δx ≈ ln(B_C/B_W)/μ.

### D8. Neutron absorbers (G3.6–G3.9)

1/v absorber: σ(E) = σ₀·sqrt(E₀/E), E₀ = 0.0253 eV. Resonance integral above E_c: RI = ∫ σ dE/E = 2·σ₀·sqrt(E₀/E_c) = 0.450·σ₀ for E_c = 0.5 eV. A tabulated RI far from 0.45·σ₀ shows non-1/v behaviour (resonance above the cut-off gives RI ≫ 0.45σ₀; a thermal resonance below the cut-off gives RI ≪ 0.45σ₀).

Macroscopic cross-section: Σ_a = N·σ_a, N = ρ·N_A·w/M (1/m³; w mass fraction).

Slab of thickness t in an isotropic flux (cosine-weighted incidence): absorption probability per incident neutron P = 1 − 2·E₃(τ), τ = Σ_a·t, E₃ the exponential integral of order 3. Small τ: P → 2τ, consistent with the Cauchy mean chord 4V/S = 2t. Per-atom self-shielding factor f(τ) = P/(2τ) ≤ 1.

Moderation: mean lethargy gain per collision ξ = 1 + ((A−1)²/(2A))·ln((A−1)/(A+1)) (ξ = 1 for A = 1); collisions from E₁ to E₂: ln(E₁/E₂)/ξ.

Water without absorber: thermal flux decays as exp(−x/L), L = sqrt(D/Σ_a). Check of Σ_a from first principles: N_H·σ_H at 2 200 m/s, times sqrt(π)/2 for a Maxwellian spectrum at 293 K.

### D9. Storage and magnets (G3.10–G3.14)

Cell reaction with n electrons at voltage V, reactant molar mass sum M (kg/mol), N_at atoms per formula: specific energy n·F·V/M (J/kg); per atom n·F·V/(N_at·N_A) (J), divide by the elementary charge for eV.

Double layer: single-electrode specific capacitance C_sp = C_dl·SSA (F/kg); symmetric cell on total electrode mass C_cell = C_sp/4; energy per kg ½·C_cell·V²; per carbon atom multiply by M_C/N_A. Graphene surface area from geometry: area per atom 3·sqrt(3)·a²/4 with a = 0.142 nm, two faces.

Energy-product ceiling: for an ideal square loop with remanence μ₀·M_r and sufficient coercivity, (BH)max = μ₀·M_r²/4.

Shape-anisotropy magnet (planner's derivation, UNSURE as a published result). Aligned infinite cylinders, volume fraction p, coherent rotation, mean-field interaction: M_r = p·M_s, H_c = (1 − p)·M_s/2. On the demagnetising branch B = μ₀·(p·M_s − H′), H′ ∈ [0, H_c]. If p ≤ 1/2 the unconstrained optimum H′ = p·M_s/2 is reachable and (BH)max = μ₀·p²·M_s²/4 ≤ μ₀·M_s²/16. If p > 1/2 the optimum is at H′ = H_c: BH = μ₀·M_s²·(3p − 1)(1 − p)/4, maximal at p = 2/3 with value μ₀·M_s²/12.

Two-phase exchange-coupled magnet (Skomski and Coey 1993, equation numbers UNSURE): f_h = μ₀·M_s²/(4·K_h); (BH)max = (μ₀·M_s²/4)·(1 − μ₀·(M_s − M_h)·M_s/(2·K_h)), M_s soft-phase and M_h hard-phase magnetisation (A/m).

Magnetic circuit with ideal iron and no leakage: H_m·l_m = −H_g·l_g, B_m·A_m = B_g·A_g, so B_g²·V_g/μ₀ = abs(B_m·H_m)·V_m and V_m = B_g²·V_g/(μ₀·abs(BH)). Hardness parameter: κ_h = sqrt(K₁/(μ₀·M_s²)); a magnet of arbitrary shape needs κ_h > 1.

### Row notes

**Note E1 (voxel conduction solve).** Unit cell of N³ voxels, N = 32. Solid voxels form three square struts of width w voxels along x, y, z through the cell centre; φ = (3w²N − 2w³)/N³. Unknown: potential at voxel centres. Face conductance between neighbours: harmonic mean of the two voxel conductivities. Boundary conditions: potential 1 and 0 on the two z faces (half-cell conductance to the face), periodic in x and y. Assemble with scipy.sparse, solve with scipy.sparse.linalg.spsolve. σ_eff = (total current through any z plane)·N/(N²·ΔV) in voxel units. Check current conservation between the first and last plane to 1e-8. Additional geometries for the sanity checks: full solid; plates parallel to z of thickness w.

**Note F6 (slab Monte Carlo).** Monoenergetic pencil beam, normal incidence on an infinite slab of areal density x. Interactions: Compton on free electrons (Klein–Nishina total cross-section × N_A·Z/A), photoelectric absorption (XCOM, log-log interpolation in energy, handled separately on each side of the W K-edge at 69.5 keV), pair production zero at E₀ ≤ 1.022 MeV. Coherent scattering and fluorescence are neglected; state this in the output. Cut-off 20 keV: deposit locally. History: sample path −ln(u)/μ_tot(E) along the direction cosine; if the photon leaves through the far face add its energy to the transmitted tally, through the near face to the reflected tally; otherwise choose the interaction by cross-section ratio. Compton sampling: sample ε = E′/E on [1/(1+2k), 1] by rejection from the Klein–Nishina energy distribution f(ε) ∝ (1/ε + ε)·(1 − ε·sin²θ/(1 + ε²)) with cos θ = 1 − (1 − ε)/(k·ε); uniform azimuth; rotate the direction. Vectorise over histories with numpy. μ in the definition of μx is the total without coherent scattering at E₀, to stay consistent with the simulated physics. Fixed random seed, recorded.

## 5. Draft G4 gate procedure (PROVISIONAL)

Unit of classification: a triple (function, operating regime, binding envelope). Outcomes: Class I (retain x), Class II (compare), out of scope.

Inputs the scenario supplies: the requirement F ≥ F_req with units and the quantity held equal; the regime variables (temperature, frequency or photon/neutron spectrum, chemical environment, duty and life); the envelope caps (mass, volume or thickness, areal density, auxiliary power) with the incumbent's usage of each; the governing equation and the coefficient c through which x acts, with c_s and c_g at the regime; process limits per material (minimum feature).

1. **Scope.** If the requirement, the binding envelope or the coefficient c cannot be stated; or no coefficient of the solid enters the governing equation (the geometry acts on a shared medium); or g and s cannot operate in the same environment and the function cannot be restated to make them comparable: **out of scope**.
2. **Threshold.** List the intensive conditions the regime imposes that contain no geometry: operating temperature against Curie, melting or decomposition temperature; chemical stability window; required edge, line or resonance energy; required specific energy against the stoichiometric bound of g. If g fails any of them: **Class I**.
3. **Compensation price.** Compute κ = c_s/c_g at the regime, on the basis of the binding cap (per mass, per volume, per area). Take n from the regime of the governing equation: 1 bulk-limited linear transport or attenuation; 1/2 penetration-limited (thick against skin depth, fin length, reaction depth); 0 saturated (black absorber); for activated kinetics use Δη = b·log₁₀κ against the allowed loss. If the price κ^n exceeds the slack (cap divided by the incumbent's usage): **Class I**.
4. **Fair comparator.** Compute the incumbent's utilisation η_u in its present form. Define s′ as the incumbent with the best admissible geometry within its own process limits (this is the thrifted-x option). Record every geometry feature used by g that s′ cannot use, with the reason.
5. **Class II.** Pass g, s′ and the duty ratio D to the Σ comparison. The gate makes no statement about which wins.

Known weak points of this draft: step 3 needs a numeric envelope, which some scenarios will not have; case 9 of note J1 can exit at step 1 or step 2; step 2 must be applied to the right coefficient (coercivity is not a threshold on K₁ alone, since shape anisotropy contributes).

## 6. Provisional G5 verdict (PROVISIONAL)

**Valid with restated scope.** The function-level dichotomy and the "transport-governed" criterion are not supported; what survives is a pre-check on (function, regime, envelope).

Restated demarcation (3 sentences):

1. A (function, regime, envelope) triple is Class I when the candidate fails an intensive threshold that no geometry enters, or when the extensive compensation κ^n that the governing equation demands does not fit inside the envelope; otherwise it is Class II.
2. In Class II, under the fairness rule, geometry cancels wherever function separates as coefficient × shape factor, so the outcome is decided by a material index c^n/(ρ·e_Σ) and by duty; geometry changes the outcome only through a truncated envelope, material-specific process limits, a switch of transport mechanism, or spatial decoupling of two functions that x couples.
3. Geometry's own general contribution is to raise the utilisation of whichever material is used towards its intrinsic per-atom limit, which reduces the amount of x and does not replace it.

What would change the verdict:

- Towards **invalid**: Row J1 shows that the gate never decides anything the constrained Σ comparison would not decide at similar cost, or the lookups show that no listed example stays Class I over a practically relevant regime (for example F2 giving a ratio below 2 already at 150 keV, or N1 ratios far smaller than remembered).
- Towards **valid as written**: F1 and F2 lookups showing W/C above about 5 at all four energies; M3 and M5 showing no feasible compensation for magnets; A3, C3, P1 and T1 all failing, so that no Class II regime pays.
- Unresolved at this stage (UNSURE): how the framework's Σ_init prices scarcity. If it is embodied exergy, G2.6 says scarce elements at small loadings are almost free in Σ and the comparison is dominated by operating dissipation; that would be a finding about the currency, not about the gate.
