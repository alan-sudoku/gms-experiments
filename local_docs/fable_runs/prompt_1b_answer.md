## 4. Verdicts

**Claim B: valid with restated scope.** The entropy expression is incomplete as usually read and, once completed, cannot rank heat sinks (B1.1–B1.6). At equal heat load, equal envelope and equal fan power, no aluminium design in the model reaches the copper plate-fin resistance: optimised Al plate fins are 9–21 % higher, pins 46–58 % higher at 0.1 W, and a gyroid would need a heat-transfer-to-friction ratio above that of a smooth channel (B2.4–B2.7). The claim survives only in the form it half-states: equal function is reachable by paying fan power. The mechanism is not larger wetted area (the Al and Cu plate optima have the same area per volume, 1225 against 1260 m²/m³ [s4g (d)]) and not a lattice. Restated claim: *for a ducted, air-cooled 100 W sink with a uniform heat source, aluminium (k = 100–200 W/m/K) plate fins of the same envelope reach the copper sink's fin-side resistance at 1.4–2.1 × the air power, about 0.15–0.42 W more fan electricity; an AM pin array or gyroid lattice is not shown to do better than plain aluminium plate fins and in a parametric model needs 2.7–4.6 × the air power; with a concentrated source an aluminium base adds 0.07–0.22 K/W that no fin geometry recovers; the comparison must be made at equal peak temperature on fan work W_fan,elec/T₀, not on the thermal entropy term.* Limits: 1-D model, ducted laminar flow, correlation constants unconfirmed, gyroid model parametric, no measurement. The lattice part of the claim is unsupported, not disproved.

**Claim C: invalid as stated.** Pt and NiFe serve different reactions in different electrolytes, so the substitution is not like-for-like (C1.1, C1.2). For oxygen evolution NiFe needs no roughness to beat Pt (C1.4). For hydrogen evolution the required roughness is 6e3–6e4 against a real Pt/C layer in alkali and 6e5–7e6 in acid, where NiFe is not stable, while the effective roughness of a porous electrode at 1 A/cm² is capped at a few hundred to about 1e4 by ionic resistance in the pores (C2.3, C2.7). The symmetric arcsinh model fixes a 118 mV/decade slope that the catalysts do not have and makes j₀ ill-defined by six orders of magnitude (C2.10, C3.1); it omits ohmic, bubble, mass-transport and durability terms that exceed the activation difference (C3.2). Restated claim: *in alkaline water electrolysis, NiFe (oxy)hydroxide on a porous Ni support replaces iridium or ruthenium oxide (not platinum) at the oxygen electrode on intrinsic activity, with about 0.27–0.47 V overpotential at 1 A/cm²; surface area lowers the overpotential by one measured Tafel slope (30–60 mV) per decade of electrochemically used area, and the used area is limited by pore-electrolyte resistance to a depth of order 0.1–0.8 mm; the comparison must be made on cell voltage and lifetime at the system level, and the case for substitution rests on scarcity, since the embodied energy of the noble metal is repaid by at most 1–2.5 mV of overpotential.* The depth range is the active depth for a 1.6 mm foam at α = 0.5 [c06].
