## Result

## Where the reviewer disagrees (0 BLOCKER, 4 MAJOR, 10 MINOR)

1. **F1, gate step 4 tests one compensation route at a time.** A fin with thickness capped at 1.5× and height at 1.05× fails each route alone but meets the requirement with both (1.018 of Cu's heat flow at 0.475 of its mass). Fix: step 4 tests joint feasibility. Then G4.3 ("the gate gives what a constrained Σ comparison gives") holds by construction: the gate is a feasibility pre-check, not an independent principle.
2. **F2, two "substitution pays" doors are not separate.** Under the fairness rule the heat pipe is compared pipe against pipe (steel wall 0.425 of Cu, k = 200 wall 0.947): series dilution, not a mechanism switch. The catalyst area route is open to the incumbent, so it reduces to the material index. Only spatial decoupling (Cu mesh vs indium oxide film) is a door the incumbent cannot use, and its film inputs are memory values. Restated sentence 2 must drop "a switch of transport mechanism".
3. **F3, G1.4's limit law is wrong for two of four problems**, at high confidence. Same-geometry penalty at κ = 2: fin 1 → 1.41; absorber slab 2 → 1 (black); porous electrode 2 → 1.41 (the only one spanning [κ^½, κ]); strip dips to 1.22 at equal thickness, exactly κ^½ = 1.28 with each metal at its own optimum. Gate step 3 is not affected (it evaluates the regime function).
4. **F4, the 13/5/0 test tally is in-sample and reading-dependent.** The gate was revised after failing on the same 18 cases; other defensible readings give 14/4/0 with a different "incomplete" set.

Minor findings that matter downstream: an H1 bug (LiCoO₂ + 3C coded as 6 atoms; 0.308 should be 0.264 eV/atom, the planner was right); the R1 example pairs recycled Al with primary Cu (symmetric routes give 0.93 and 0.65, and two of seven pairs flip within a 15–17 % change of the embodied ratio); embodied values are primary energy while operating losses are delivered electricity (a factor of order 2–3, UNSURE).
