## Where the reviewer disagrees (0 BLOCKER, 4 MAJOR, 13 MINOR)

1. **#1, concurrent loads.** "894 / 268 at s = 1" is the slow case alone; the minimum rule's real error is 100 to 1.87 at s = 1, worst 318 / 97 near s ≈ 0.8. The direction and the conclusion (cycle counting needed) stand; the magnitude in the verdict is wrong by 9 to 480 times. S2's `b4` output already showed it (orchestrator checked: `b4_b5_concurrent.txt` lines 19–22).
2. **#2, section loss.** "At most 4.38 / 6.77" covers linear recession under axial load only. Bending or torsion of a round member (p = 3) gives 8.91; parabolic recession gives 25.5, and 42.2 / 33.0 with p = 3, above the low end of the F_env range. "Section loss is always smaller than the environment effect" holds only for linear recession and p ≤ 2.
3. **#3, Rule W omits pit-initiated fatigue.** A 3 mm wall with pitting factor 3 at 10 µm/yr: Rule W gives 12.7 yr, the answer's own pit model 2.86 yr (4.45 times non-conservative; 2.42 at b = −0.124). Perforation is an upper bound on life, not a conservative cap. The pit model's governing mode also depends on an unstated ΔK convention at R = −1 (#12).
4. **#4, effect of b.** Up to 84 %, not 40 %. Conclusion (no default changes) stands.

Minor findings that matter downstream: the Rule R flag band for concurrent hysteresis (0.7 to 1.1) is in-sample and "well separated frequencies" needs a number (#13); Lazan's exponent n may exceed 2 near the endurance level (UNSURE, snippet), which would tighten the Rule R gate to x_e ≤ 0.007 for a round strut (#15); the C14 null-case expectation was wrong for ρ ≥ 1 (#8); C15 "not recomputed" direction is wrong (lives get shorter) (#9); the E4 margin 0.181 uses the lower-bound life where the exact one gives 0.244 (#17); one identity check counted as evidence in M3.4 (#10).
