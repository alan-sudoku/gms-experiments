## Decisive check (item 3): Szargut's CExC (K1–K3)

Note on source level: K1 (Szargut, Morris & Steward 1988) and K3 (Szargut & Morris 1987) are paywalled/book-only and were **not** read directly. K2 (Szargut 2005, *Exergy Method: Technical and Ecological Applications*) front matter, TOC, Chapter 1 (`8.6`), and Chapter 3 excerpts (`7.1.3`, `16.2.2`, `16.3.3`, `26.1.1`, `28.3.3`) **were** read directly as primary text for K2, supplemented by secondary descriptions of K1/K3.

1. **Does CExC include the ore's own exergy?**
   - **Yes.**
   - *Primary text (K2, Szargut 2005, Ch. 3 §3.3):* Szargut states that the CExC balance equation equals the CExC burdening semi-finished products and by-products plus *"the exergy of natural resources extracted from nature in the considered link"* (`26.1.1`), and includes raw mineral inputs such as imported iron ore directly in the Chapter 3 input–output system (`16.2.2`, `28.3.3`).
   - *Secondary sources (describing K1/K3):* Multiple secondary studies explicitly confirm that CExC (Szargut & Morris 1987; Szargut et al. 1988) counts the chemical exergy of non-energetic raw materials extracted from nature (e.g., iron ore, copper ore) relative to the reference environment as the starting benchmark (`19.3.1`, `19.3.3`, `25.4.1`, `28.3.5`).

2. **Which by-product allocation rule does CExC use?**
   - **Avoided-expenditure / "replaced process" substitution rule** (crediting the CExC of the major product of another process displaced by the by-product, using a replacement ratio `s_{iu}`).
   - *Primary text (K2, Szargut 2005, Ch. 3 §3.3):* Szargut eliminates by-product unknowns by substituting the CExC of the major product *"replaced by a unit of the u-th by-product"*, giving Eq. (3.8): `b*_j = Σ (f_{ij} - a_{ij}) b*_i + β_j` (`7.1.3`, `16.3.3`), illustrated in Ch. 3 by coke-oven gas substituting imported natural gas (`16.2.2`).
   - *Secondary sources (for K1/K3):* Stanek / Szargut-school papers (`7.1.2`, `7.1.7`, `7.1.8`, `16.3.6`) confirm this "principle of avoided expenditures" / "replaced processes" across Szargut (1987, 1988, 2005).

3. **Does CExC include capital goods (machines and installations)?**
   - **Optional in principle (Levels 3–4), omitted in standard practice (Levels 1–2).**
   - *Primary text (K2, Szargut 2005, Ch. 3 §3.3):* Szargut writes Eq. (3.9) showing how *"The consumption of machines and installations can be also taken into account in eqn (3.8)"* via `a_{ij} = m_{ij} / [τ_i G_j (1 - u_i)]` (Level 3: machines/installations for Level 1; Level 4: machines for machines), but explicitly states: **"The analysis of cumulative indices finishes usually on the second level."** (`7.1.3`, `16.3.3`).
   - *Secondary sources (for K1/K3):* Cornelissen (1997, citing Szargut 1988) confirms *"The CExC is determined to the second level, i.e. the irreversibility associated with the production of the plants themselves are not taken into account"* (`16.2.3`), and ACS *Energy & Fuels* (2004, citing Szargut & Morris 1987) likewise notes that in standard CExC applications *"the cumulative exergy contribution of the equipment is omitted"* (`13.1.9`).
